# Friendly prediction/betting among friends: market, legal, and platform landscape (August 2026)

## 1. The apps: who is alive, who is dead, and why

Real-money "bet your friends" apps have almost all died, been acquired, or converted into regulated exchanges.

- **Betcha (2019–21)** — DFS-style props; acquired by Vivid Seats Dec 2021 ($25M + earnout), rebranded Vivid Picks, shut to new play in 2025. The name has since been recycled; the relevant one is **"Betcha: Dumb Bets with Friends" (2026, iOS/Android)**: no money, voice-to-bet, share-to-iMessage/WhatsApp with one-tap join, a running scoreboard and head-to-head "nemesis" records. Positioning: "not a sportsbook, not a prediction market — no house, no odds." That is a near-exact description of the category Chiang Pai is in.
- **Wager (wager.app)** — no-money social bets; tiny.
- **WagerLab** — the best-documented no-money survivor: virtual units, $800K pre-seed (Stadia Ventures, 2023), "1 millionth game"; monetises via sponsored cash-prize contests and sportsbook affiliate. Still small after 5+ years — the niche has produced no breakout.
- **Dare / Xdares, Klash** — challenge apps spike on novelty and churn.
- **Novig** — P2P sweepstakes → CFTC-registered DCM June 2026, $75M Series B. **ProphetX** — same path, CFTC approval June 2026. **Sleeper** — fantasy + chat; Sleeper Markets NFA-approved Jan 2026. Its sticky core was never money: it is the group chat + league.
- **Polymarket / Kalshi** — social layer is referrals, comments, profile P&L; no "bet a friend" primitive. Polymarket US iOS app Dec 2025. No native paper-trading mode; a cottage industry of simulators fills it.
- **Futuur** — play-money "Ooms" plus Curaçao-licensed real money; uses the play-money tier to find superforecasters.
- **India: Probo and TradeX** — opinion trading at ₹1–10. Probo raided by the ED (July 2025, ₹284.5 cr frozen), shut real-money ops on 21 Aug 2025 the moment the Rajya Sabha passed the bill; TradeX shut the same week. Killer: the law, with GST (28% → 40% on face value) and PMLA enforcement as accelerants.
- **Dream11** — paid contests were ~95% of revenue. Pivoted to free-to-play with ads/sponsorship Sept 2025 (~10M DAU claimed); by Dec 2025 announced it was exiting fantasy as the core to become a "second-screen sports entertainment" platform. Even the best-capitalised operator did not believe free-to-play fantasy alone retains at scale.

## 2. Do play-money prediction games retain?

**A thin core retains hard; the mass does not, unless the game is embedded in an existing social group or a competitive season.**

- **Manifold** — ran real-money "sweepcash" Sep 2024–Mar 2025, killed it; back to mana-only. Daily active traders hit record lows of ~880 (2025) vs ~2,000+ in early 2024. What keeps it alive: leagues with promotion/demotion, streaks, "Wrapped" summaries, sponsored tournaments, comment culture.
- **Metaculus** — no currency at all; reputation plus sponsor tournaments. Retention driver is track record/status.
- **Reddit Predictions** (2021) — quietly disappeared. Platform-feature prediction games without a persistent group die.
- **Fantasy leagues** — the one mass-market proof that play-for-nothing retains for decades. Ingredients: a fixed group of known people, a season with a calendar, a draft (ritual), a commissioner, a trophy/punishment for last place.

What keeps no-money games alive (Duolingo/Strava/Snap data):
- **Leagues with promotion/demotion** — Duolingo leaderboards raised learning time 17% and tripled highly engaged users.
- **Winnable, segmented competition** — Strava's segment leaderboards among people you ride with retain; global ones hurt.
- **Dyadic loss-aversion** — Snap streaks; group streaks Sept 2025.
- **Social stakes over financial stakes** — Betcha's scoreboard + nemesis records, fantasy "loser punishment", "loser buys dinner". Status stakes the app records but does not enforce — which is exactly what keeps them legal.

## 3. Legal

### India — Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 (PROGA)
- Passed 21 Aug 2025; Rules notified 22 Apr 2026, in force **1 May 2026**; Online Gaming Authority of India under MeitY.
- **Online money game** (s.2(1)(g)): any online game played by paying fees, depositing money **or other stakes** in expectation of winning money or other stakes.
- **"Other stakes"** (s.2(1)(j)): anything equivalent or convertible to money, including credits, coins, tokens, real or virtual, **which is purchased by paying money directly or by indirect means**. The hinge word is *purchased*: a token nobody buys and nobody can cash out is not a stake.
- **Online social game**: for entertainment/recreation/skill development, no staking in expectation of monetary gain; a **subscription or one-time access fee is expressly not a stake**. Under the 2026 Rules social games get deemed approval; registration is voluntary unless the Centre notifies a category.
- **Offences**: offering money games up to 3 yrs / ₹1 cr; advertising 2 yrs / ₹50 lakh; banks/payment facilitation 3 yrs / ₹1 cr; cognizable and non-bailable. The *user* is not criminalised; the operator and the payment rail are.
- **Courts**: constitutional challenges consolidated before a three-judge SC bench, pending. 27 May 2026 (Gameskraft GST): real-money games on uncertain outcomes are "betting and gambling" for GST regardless of skill — nothing in it touches no-money play.
- **Exposure of a play-money app whose users settle side bets offline**: low on the statute's text — no fee, no coin sales, no cash-out, pies not "purchased". Exposure rises if (a) the app ever sells pies or anything convertible, (b) it records, enforces or facilitates an off-app money settlement (a "loser pays ₹500" field, UPI deep links on a prediction, IOU tracking), (c) it advertises with money language.

### United States
No federal issue for no-money play. Real-money betting between friends is governed by state "social gambling" exceptions (bona fide social relationship, no house cut); ~a dozen states have none. The app takes no rake and holds no money, so it is at most a scorekeeper.

### UK
Gambling Act 2005: a prediction competition is betting **only if payment is required to enter**; genuine free-entry games are outside the Commission's remit. Caveat: a paid subscription to an app that is mainly a prediction game can be argued to be "payment to enter".

### EU
No harmonisation. Germany criminalises unlicensed event betting; Malta is building a prediction-market framework. Universal safe harbour: **no stake, no gambling**.

## 4. Platform policy

**Apple** — Guideline 5.3 on real-money gaming. Age ratings: **Gambling** (real money or currency exchangeable for it) → 18+; **Simulated Gambling** (wagering with non-convertible currency) → 13+ infrequent, **18+ frequent** (June 2025 tightening); **Contests** (rankings/rewards) → 4+/13+. Risk: a reviewer who sees "bet"/"stake"/"pool" and a currency may tag *Simulated Gambling – Frequent* (18+) and ask why there is no licence. Betcha's "no house, no odds" copy is a defensive rewrite against exactly this.

**Google Play** — real-money gambling needs per-territory licences; India is no longer a permitted RMG market post-PROGA. Simulated gambling is allowed but rated via IARC; ad networks restrict it.

**PWA** — sidesteps both stores' review and rating entirely. For a no-money app the PWA route carries no legal downside, only a distribution/UX one. It does not change the law: PROGA binds the operator, not the store.

## 5. Product patterns for stakes without money

Duolingo leagues (weekly cohort, promotion/demotion); Strava segments (local, winnable); Snap streaks (loss-averse commitment); BeReal (synchronised moment); Wordle share grid (a result card that fits a chat thread); fantasy (draft ritual, commissioner, season, last-place punishment); Betcha 2026 (voice-entered bet, share card, nemesis records); Dream11 post-ban (moved the stake from money to being there live with people).

## Implications

**Can a play-money prediction game be the hook for a friend-trip app?** Yes, with a caveat: play money by itself does not retain strangers, but retains very well inside a **closed group with a season**. A trip is a natural season — fixed roster, start and end, a daily stream of cheap resolvable questions, and a final leaderboard that is also the story of the trip. That is the fantasy-league shape, not the Manifold shape.

**Safest framing — three rules:**
1. Nothing in the app is ever purchased or cashed out. No IAP for pies, no "top-up", no sponsor cash prizes. Any subscription must be clearly for the app, not for entry to a contest.
2. The app must not *facilitate* a money settlement on a prediction: no rupee/dollar fields, no UPI/Venmo links, no IOU ledger. Let the group settle in dinner or dares; if a stake is recorded at all, record it as free-text social forfeit.
3. Vocabulary: *prediction, call, pie, leaderboard*; avoid *wager, stake (money sense), odds, payout, cash*. Answer store questionnaires as *Contests*, not *Simulated Gambling*. Consider presenting pies as points rather than a currency.

**Platform.** Ship as a **PWA first**: no store review, no age-rating questionnaire, no 5.3 rejection risk, no legal difference. Go to the stores only when iOS push or discoverability is the measured blocker; submit as a social/Contests app; expect Google Play easier than Apple. Never pursue the Polymarket/Kalshi path or any sweepstakes model — in India those are closed.
