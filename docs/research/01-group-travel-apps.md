## 3. Why WhatsApp + Sheets + Splitwise still wins

- **Install asymmetry.** One planner is motivated; 5–10 others are not. WhatsApp is installed; a Sheet opens from a link. Every dedicated app pays an onboarding tax per member per trip. "The most common group failure isn't picking the wrong app, it's three people using three different ones."
- **Trips are episodic.** A $40/yr per-seat subscription is wrong-shaped for something that happens twice a year.
- **Bolted-on sharing.** "No single app was designed to hold a group trip together — they were built for one person, with sharing bolted on later."
- **Money needs a rail, not a ledger.** Splitwise records; settlement happens on UPI/Venmo/cash anyway.
- **Decisions happen in chat.** "Someone posts a hotel link and it gets buried under 200 messages about dinner."

## 4. What friend groups actually complain about

Quantified (Experian, June 2025, n>700 US adults who travelled with friends):
- Only **1 in 4** friend groups set a budget upfront.
- **>50%** of Gen Z/millennials have had a money disagreement on a friends' trip; **1 in 5 ended a friendship** over it.
- Gen Z/millennials most likely to go **50%+ over budget**; top stressors: unexpected costs, splitting fairly when priorities differ, pressure to spend.

Credit Karma via Fortune (July 2025): 38% of Gen Z/millennials have taken on debt for wedding/bachelor(ette) trips; 56% feel obligated to go; ~40% have a friend who drives them to overspend.

Qualitative themes (Reddit AITA/AIO, substacks, press): the planner's resentment ("researched, booked and organised everything for 16 people" then hijacked); scope creep (friends add partners without asking); freeloading ("refused to split shared costs despite agreeing to the budget"); silence in the chat ("'when works for everyone?' … 'let me check', 'maybe April?'"); mismatched pace; budget exclusion. Complaints cluster on **money fairness and awkwardness**, **decision paralysis / flakiness**, and **one person carrying the load** — not on lack of itinerary features.

## 5. Market sizing

- US: 24% of travellers expect to travel with friends in 2025; Airbnb: >80% of bookings are group trips; 79% of Gen Z/millennials call leisure travel a priority (Amex 2026).
- **India outbound: record 32.71M departures in 2025 (+5.9%)**; ~50M by 2030; market USD 23.4B in 2026 at ~11% CAGR.
- **Thailand: 2.49M Indian arrivals in 2025 (+16.8%) while total Thai arrivals fell 7.2%**; 3rd largest market; visa-free (60-day, being restructured to 30).
- **Vietnam: ~746k Indian arrivals 2025 (+48.9%)**, 6th source; $25 90-day e-visa.
- **Sri Lanka: 531k+ Indian arrivals 2025 (+27.5%)**, largest source; free ETA.
- **UAE/Dubai: 2.4M Indian visitors to Dubai (2024), 3.5M to UAE**; India the top market.
- **Bali: ~569k Indian arrivals 2025**, #2 source market; VoA.
- Japan 315k (+35%). Declines: US, Maldives, Azerbaijan, Turkey.
- Visa-free/VoA for Indians now covers Thailand, Malaysia, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, Maldives, Mauritius, Philippines (June 2025), Indonesia VoA, Vietnam e-visa.

Take: the Indian-friend-group-to-SE-Asia trip is the fastest-growing, most price-sensitive, most WhatsApp/UPI-native segment in the world, and no app above is built for it.

## 6. Monetisation patterns that worked

1. **Per-trip, one-pays-for-all** (Settle Up): matches the episodic shape and the reality that the planner pays and nobody else installs. Low ARPU, zero coordination tax.
2. **Freemium with a usage cap** (Splitwise): financially successful but burned brand equity and spawned a competitor category in 18 months.
3. **Loss-leader for a financial product** (Tricount → bunq; Airbnb; JetBlue/Troupe): the ledger is worth more as distribution than as revenue.
4. **Keepsake + affiliate, no subscription** (Polarsteps): memories monetise better than planning.
5. **Per-seat annual Pro** (Wanderlog, TripIt, Lambus): works for frequent solo travellers, fails for groups — the pattern every group app copied and none scaled with.
6. **B2B2C via agencies/airlines**: sustains products, rarely grows them.
7. **Booking take-rate**: needs volume group apps don't have.

## Implications for a new entrant

- **Do not build another itinerary app.** Free incumbents, Airbnb absorbing the category, SEO saturated by AI-wrapper entrants.
- **Do not build another pure split app** unless UPI-native and India-first; that lane is crowded and Paytm now does it free.
- **The unsolved pain is social, not logistical**: awkwardness about money, flakiness, the planner carrying everyone, the post-trip "who owes whom". The wedge is a *mechanism that changes behaviour inside the group* — commitment devices, stakes, visible accountability, a game — which the spreadsheet cannot do and nothing listed attempts.
- **Ship inside WhatsApp, not beside it.** Link-joinable, no-install for members, one owner; guests see the page before joining.
- **Price per trip, charged to one person**, or take no money from the group and monetise a keepsake/affiliate layer. Per-seat annual subscriptions are the pattern that has failed this category repeatedly.
- **Pick the Indian outbound SE-Asia group as the beachhead**: 32.7M departures, Thailand +17%, Vietnam +49%, visa friction collapsing, UPI-settled, WhatsApp-organised, multi-currency by default, zero purpose-built incumbents.
- **Beware absorption** by Airbnb, Google Maps, and WhatsApp itself. The defensible part is the opinionated social mechanic and the group's own history.
